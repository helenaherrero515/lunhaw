# Rebuild Lunhaw's Green Index on canopy targets

Lunhaw can publish a defensible 0–100 Green Index for its 26 parks and libraries, but not with EQ-001 as written. The WHO source it cites gives only an *access* rule of thumb (0.5–1 ha of public green space within 300 m of homes), not canopy, NDVI or permeability weights. Inside a park polygon, its canopy (C) and NDVI (V) terms both measure the same trees, so roughly 80% of the weight sits on one signal. Its permeability term (P) counts vegetation a third time and gives water no credit, even though water is the strongest park-cooling driver measured in Metro Manila. The recommended replacement, called **EQ-002** here, scores mutually exclusive land-cover fractions within a frozen spatial unit: `GI = 0.60·Cs + 0.20·Os + 0.20·Us`. Cs is tree canopy measured against a 30% target and capped at 100. Os is other vegetation plus water against the same target. Us is the unsealed share of ground. NDVI becomes a displayed context metric, not a scored term. Parks are scored on their own polygon; libraries on a 300 m land-only catchment with a separate WHO access flag. The inputs are all open and allow commercial use: Meta/WRI CHMv2 1 m canopy height (CC BY 4.0), Sentinel-2 L2A from Microsoft Planetary Computer, ESA WorldCover 2021 (CC BY 4.0), Google Open Buildings (choose CC BY 4.0) and OpenStreetMap polygons (ODbL). A Python batch job on a free GitHub Actions runner can process them for near-zero cost. Under Lunhaw's glass-box rules every component and every composite is an **Estimate**. "Verified" is reserved for facts copied from an authoritative document. Before any of this ships, the existing seed data must be removed: migration `supabase/migrations/00012_seed_green_index.sql` marks the simulated canopy dataset with `status = 'VERIFIED'` and attributes it to Copernicus, which breaks the app's own integrity rules today.

## EQ-001 fails on its citation, on double-counting and on Manila's low canopy

The citation is the first thing to fix. The 2017 WHO Europe brief contains one quantitative rule: *"urban residents should be able to access public green spaces of at least 0.5–1 hectare within 300 metres' linear distance (around 5 minutes' walk) of their homes."* Everything else in it is qualitative: visibility, safety, maintenance, and choosing monitoring indicators at project start. It sets no greenness metric and no weights ([WHO Europe 2017](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/ac6c76f4-7840-404f-8b8b-1ce3de666bc1/content)). The often-repeated "WHO 9 m² of green space per capita" figure has no traceable WHO primary publication. A 2025 review notes the indicator was removed for exactly that reason ([Frontiers in Sustainable Cities 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-cities/articles/10.3389/frsc.2025.1542464/pdf)). Philippine papers still attribute it to WHO ([Francisco et al. 2026](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-5-W4-2025/237/2026/isprs-annals-X-5-W4-2025-237-2026.pdf)). Lunhaw should cite WHO only for the 300 m catchment and the 0.5 ha size flag, and should never show "WHO recommends 9 m²". The 2016 WHO evidence review was not retrieved, so it cannot be quoted yet.

The structural problem is double-counting. Canopy cover and NDVI measure different things. Canopy is the 2D crown area, a proxy for shade. NDVI is the spectral greenness of *all* vegetation, grass included, and it saturates over closed canopy. Across US neighbourhoods the two correlate only moderately, **r ≈ 0.3–0.4**. Canopy was the best single predictor of perceived greenness ([IJERPH 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17186501)). In Toronto, satisfaction with urban trees tracked canopy and eye-level visibility, and NDVI was not significant ([npj Urban Sustainability 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42949-023-00119-8)). Inside a park polygon, though, a high-canopy park is also a high-NDVI park. NDVI's only extra information is credit for lawn. The Quezon City evidence suggests lawn barely helps with heat: dense continuous canopy is about **9.6–9.8 °C cooler (land-surface temperature) than built-up land**, while fragmented canopy shows **+0.09 °C** and sparse vegetation **+1.55 °C** UHI ([Tobias 2026](https://iesm.science.upd.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Abstract_Tobias.pdf)). EQ-001 gives that lawn credit 30% of the score.

P has the same overlap problem and misses water entirely. Permeable ground is mostly vegetated ground, so C + V + P counts vegetation three times. Meanwhile, across 25 Metro Manila parks the strongest correlates of park cooling intensity were *water* metrics (aggregation index of water r = 0.421, water share r = 0.368). Mean park cooling was **4.72 °C LST (range 0.68–8.15 °C)** ([Guadalupe et al. 2026](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-5-W4-2025/285/2026/isprs-annals-X-5-W4-2025-285-2026.pdf)). Imperviousness is still a legitimate signal. In Bangkok, Jakarta and Manila it correlates strongly with surface temperature, about 3 °C warmer than green space on average ([Estoque et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2016.12.151), snippet). Concrete is also cited as a cause of Metro Manila flash floods ([Letran repository](https://research-manila.letran.edu.ph/download/125), snippet). So P's role should be "how much ground is sealed", measured as its own fraction.

Raw percentages also squash Manila into the bottom of the scale. Manila City is **86.25% built-up and 8.45% vegetation** ([Francisco et al. 2026](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-5-W4-2025/237/2026/isprs-annals-X-5-W4-2025-237-2026.pdf)). NCR-wide canopy estimates run from about 4% (Global Forest Watch, 30% density threshold) to 8.8–10% (Sentinel-2), depending on definition ([Leaves.PH](https://leaves.ph/report), snippet). A score built on raw canopy percent would put nearly every library catchment between 0 and 30. That is not wrong, but it hides the differences residents care about.

No established index derives canopy-vs-NDVI-vs-permeability weights from outcome evidence. Tree Equity Score uses a single canopy indicator scored against a goal, plus seven *equally weighted* priority variables ([Tree Equity Score methodology](https://treeequityscore.org/methodology), snippet). The Manila Green Index explicitly chose *"equal weights… as a compromise"* ([Francisco et al. 2026](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-5-W4-2025/237/2026/isprs-annals-X-5-W4-2025-237-2026.pdf)). The OECD/JRC handbook treats weighting as a modelling choice that requires robustness and sensitivity analysis ([Saisana, JRC](https://user2006.r-project.org/Slides/Huergo+Saisana.pdf); [OECD handbook](https://www.oecd.org/economics/handbook-on-constructing-composite-indicators_533411815016)). The honest position follows: any weights Lunhaw picks are a **convention**, and the app should say so.

## EQ-002 scores four exclusive cover fractions against a 30% canopy target

### The formula

Within each spatial unit (land area only; open water such as Manila Bay is excluded), the pipeline assigns every 1 m or 10 m cell to exactly one class, using the precedence **water > tree canopy > building > paved > other vegetation > bare/unsealed**. This gives fractions T (tree), G (other vegetation), W (water), S (sealed = buildings + paved) and B (bare) that sum to 100%. Double-counting becomes impossible by construction.

| Term | Definition | Normalisation | Weight | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cs, canopy | T = share of unit with canopy height ≥ 2 m (≥ 5 m stored as a sensitivity variant), building footprints masked out | `min(100, 100·T/0.30)` | 0.60 | Canopy has the strongest evidence for perceived greenness ([IJERPH 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17186501)) and for cooling ([Tobias 2026](https://iesm.science.upd.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Abstract_Tobias.pdf)). The 30% target comes from 3-30-300 ([Konijnendijk 2023](https://journal.hep.com.cn/jfr/EN/10.1007/s11676-022-01523-z)). The goal-and-cap approach mirrors Tree Equity Score. |
| Os, other green and blue | G + W, where G = non-tree pixels with dry-season median NDVI ≥ 0.3 | `min(100, 100·(G+W)/0.30)` | 0.20 | Gives credit for lawns and shrubs, and for water, which drives park cooling in Manila ([Guadalupe et al. 2026](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-5-W4-2025/285/2026/isprs-annals-X-5-W4-2025-285-2026.pdf)). The 0.3 NDVI cut and the 30% target are Lunhaw conventions to calibrate during validation. |
| Us, unsealed ground | 1 − S | `100·(1 − S)` | 0.20 | Sealing is linked to heat and flooding in Manila ([Estoque et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2016.12.151), snippet; [Letran](https://research-manila.letran.edu.ph/download/125), snippet). It also differentiates places that have already passed the canopy target. |
| NDVI | Zonal mean of the dry-season median NDVI, with p10 and p90 | Displayed only: `100·clip(NDVI/0.8, 0, 1)` with fixed anchors | 0 | Kept as "vegetation vigour" context and as a QA check, so tree cover isn't counted twice. |

The 0.60/0.20/0.20 split is an *evidence-informed ranking, not an evidence-derived number*. Canopy gets the most weight because its links to shade, cooling, perception and an external target are the best documented. The two other terms are equally weighted, following the Manila Green Index and Tree Equity Score practice of equal weights as an explicit compromise. Us still correlates with T + G, since unsealed ground is mostly green. EQ-002 accepts that overlap knowingly: the canopy term saturates at the 30% target, and Us answers a separate question ("how much of this place is concrete?"). The sensitivity analysis below tests whether the overlap changes any band.

As an illustration only (not a real place), consider a unit with T = 12%, G = 8%, W = 2% and S = 60%. Then Cs = 40, Os = 33 and Us = 40, giving GI ≈ 38.7, which displays as "about 40, borderline Low/Moderate".

### Spatial unit: one formula, two units

**Parks are scored on their own polygon.** Use pinned OpenStreetMap IDs (or the DENR-NCR parks list that Guadalupe et al. combined with OSM). Show park size as a separate attribute, and flag parks under **0.5 ha**, the WHO lower bound, as lower confidence. Metro Manila's cooling studies found diminishing returns at about **2–4 ha** for cooling intensity and about **14 ha** for efficiency ([Guadalupe et al. 2026](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-5-W4-2025/285/2026/isprs-annals-X-5-W4-2025-285-2026.pdf)). That supports displaying size next to the score rather than folding it into the score.

**Libraries are scored on a 300 m land-only Euclidean catchment**, minus the library's own footprint. The 300 m matches WHO's access rule, the "300" of 3-30-300, the 300 m grid of the Manila Green Index, and park cooling distances of roughly 150–300 m found elsewhere ([Landscape Ecology 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10980-025-02162-5), snippet). Next to the score, libraries get a yes/no access flag: "public green space ≥ 0.5 ha within 300 m". A 5-minute walking-network isochrone is a later upgrade. Euclidean buffers ignore gated villages, flyovers and fences. In Surakarta, park access could rise from ~25% to 79% of buildings if green spaces were fenceless and high quality ([UFUG 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1618866725001311), snippet).

Because the two units answer different questions, the app should word them differently. Parks get "How green is this park?" and libraries get "How green is the area within a 5-minute walk?". Park and library scores should not be ranked against each other. An optional 100–150 m surroundings ring around parks can be shown as a second number. It should not be blended into v1.

### Public communication

Display the score **rounded to the nearest 5**, in bands **Low (0–39), Moderate (40–69), High (70–100)**, with its uncertainty range, for example "about 40 (likely 30–50)". Show each component next to its target, for example "Tree canopy 12% · target 30%". Two fixed caption lines carry the glass-box story. The first: "Weights are a Lunhaw convention, not a scientific standard; canopy is weighted most because tree shade is the best-evidenced driver of cooling and perceived greenness." The second follows EPA EJScreen's caveat style: "Satellite-based screening estimate; may miss small trees, recent changes or paved areas; not an official inventory" ([USDA RD EJ appendix quoting EJScreen](https://www.rd.usda.gov/sites/default/files/appendix_h_environmental_j_screens_508.pdf)). Never attach temperature (°C), health or "WHO-approved" claims to the score. The cooling literature measures land-surface temperature, which correlates only marginally with human thermal comfort ([Frontiers in Earth Science 2023](https://frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1133901/text), snippet), and the index does not measure temperature at all.

Resolution matters more than anything else in this table. A 1 ha park is only about **100 WorldCover pixels**, many of them mixed edge pixels, but about **10,000 CHM pixels**. That is why CHMv2 ranks first despite having no Manila validation. All seven named parks checked live have OSM `leisure=park` features. Arroceros Forest Park is about **2.12 ha**, Paco Park **1.17 ha**, Ayala Triangle Gardens **5.30 ha** and Washington SyCip Park **1.00 ha**, measured as planar approximations from OSM ([Overpass API](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)). Rizal Park is tagged "Parque Rizal" (way 24159887). A naive Nominatim search for it returns a Las Piñas neighbourhood, so the IDs must be pinned. Marikina River Park is fragmented across three ways and needs unioning. The other 19 places were not checked.

On the OSM licence: publishing only computed scores is generally treated as an ODbL "produced work". Publishing the boundary polygons as a downloadable database would trigger share-alike. The ODbL and OSMF guidance pages were not fetched, so this needs confirmation before launch.

## A free Python batch job turns six inputs into provenance-tagged scores

The pipeline runs offline once a year and stores only derived numbers, so the website never touches a raster at request time. The stack is Python 3.12 with `pystac-client`, `planetary-computer`, `odc-stac`, `xarray`/`rioxarray`, `rasterio`, `geopandas`, `exactextract`, `osmnx` and `psycopg`, pinned in a lock file. It runs on a GitHub Actions standard runner, which is free in public repos (4 CPU, 16 GB RAM). Private repos get 2 CPU and 8 GB, plus $12/month of included compute ([GitHub runners](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/runners/github-hosted-runners); [billing](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/product-billing/github-actions)). The data volume is megabytes. The 5–20 minute runtime is an estimate, not a benchmark.

Cloud handling is the real tropical difficulty. Over Manila, a CopPhil query for 1 Jan–30 Sep 2024 found **20 Sentinel-2 L2A products, of which only 6 had ≤ 20% cloud** ([CopPhil knowledge base](https://knowledgebase.infra.copphil.philsa.gov.ph/en/latest/gettingstarted/Use-CopPhil-JupyterLab-and-Python-to-find-Sentinel-2-Level-2-products-over-Manila-in-2024.html)). Tropical clouds and haze can hide the surface for "up to several months" ([Data in Brief 2021](https://doaj.org/article/4cad01827ab445b68814144528374e00)). Ground-camera data show wet-season median cloud above 40% and dry-season median below it ([Manila Observatory](https://archium.ateneo.edu/manila-observatory/6), indicative only). The pipeline therefore requires **at least 3 clear observations per pixel and a per-place mean `n_clear` ≥ 3**. Below that, it falls back to a full-year median and flags `low_obs`. Cloud Score+ (`cs_cdf` ≥ 0.6) is a better mask, but it lives in Earth Engine ([EE catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_CLOUD_SCORE_PLUS_V1_S2_HARMONIZED)), so it is out of scope for a monetisable stack. If SCL masking proves leaky, CDSE openEO (10,000 free credits per month, about 8 credits per 10 ha job) is the free fallback ([CDSE openEO credits](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/APIs/openEO/credit_usage.html)).

Canopy is static between product releases. That is acceptable, but the data year must appear in the UI next to every canopy number. The CHM imagery date is genuinely unclear. The v1 paper reports Maxar imagery from **2017–2020** ([Tolan et al. 2024, arXiv 2304.07213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07213v3)), while the v2 registry credits "© 2016 Vantor". Lunhaw should read the per-tile observation-date GeoJSON and show that date rather than either credit line.

## Every number ships as an Estimate until a person checks it against imagery

### Validation a two-person team can run

The defensible near-zero-cost method is a probability-sample photo-interpretation check, following Olofsson et al.'s good practice: probability sampling, documented response design, and confidence intervals ([Olofsson et al. 2014](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/44846/)). For each frozen unit, generate random points in **i-Tree Canopy** (recommended specifically for the Philippines at a Palawan workshop) or **Collect Earth Online** ([i-Tree Philippines workshop](https://www.itreetools.org/support/resources-overview/project-profiles/i-tree-news-article-i-tree-and-stew-map-workshop-focuses-rapid-growth-philippines); [CEO](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170012158/downloads/20170012158.pdf)). Label each point Tree / Other vegetation / Water / Sealed / Bare on the most recent clear high-resolution image, and record the image date. Then compare the labelled proportions with EQ-002's fractions for the same unit.

i-Tree reports each class as p ± SE, where SE = √(p(1−p)/n). For example, a 562-point report gave tree cover 27.22% ± 1.88 ([Quartzsite report](https://desing.itreetools.org/documents/731/LBarnettWaren_and_CMares_Quartzsite_Canopy_Report_2015.pdf), snippet). A ±5 pp 95% CI needs about **385 points at p = 0.5, 246 at p = 0.2 and 139 at p = 0.1** (standard binomial arithmetic). A peer-reviewed benchmark found estimates stabilise by about 100 points, but an SE under 1.7% needs more than 500 ([Arboriculture & Urban Forestry 46(1):51](https://auf.isa-arbor.com/content/46/1/51)). A practical plan for 26 places is 100 points each (about 2,600 points, roughly ±10 pp), rising to 300–400 points for flagship parks. A second person relabels 10–20% of points to measure interpreter agreement.

A component is accepted when **|product − photo-interpreted| ≤ 10 pp on at least 80% of places**, a Lunhaw convention. On the first run, use the labelled points to calibrate the CHM height threshold (2 m vs 5 m) and the NDVI ≥ 0.3 cut. The imagery is only for labelling: publish your own labels and percentages, not screenshots or tiles. Esri's terms permit tracing and validation for vector creation ([OSM wiki: Esri](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Esri), secondhand). Current Google Earth terms were not retrieved.

Benchmarks provide sanity bounds, not validation. The 25-park ISPRS dataset is the best per-park cross-check ([Guadalupe et al. 2026](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-5-W4-2025/285/2026/isprs-annals-X-5-W4-2025-285-2026.pdf)). Library catchments should mostly land between single digits and about 20% tree cover; wooded parks such as Arroceros or Quezon Memorial Circle should be far higher. Outliers trigger a manual look. Metro-wide figures disagree by definition. DENR's 12,152.79 ha of green space is about **21% of NCR** ([Inquirer 2019](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1133654/metro-manilas-green-spaces-continue-to-shrink)), while tree area is "a little over 3%" ([Earth Journalism Network](https://earthjournalism.net/stories/blazing-heat-of-2024-ignites-filipinos-need-for-trees-and-green-spaces), snippet). This disagreement is itself a reason to show definitions. It is also a warning that global products can diverge sharply: eight forest maps of the Philippines estimated national forest cover anywhere from **23% to 67%** ([NIES](https://www.nies.go.jp/social/publications/abstract/simultaneous_comparison_and_assessment_of_eight_remotely_sensed_maps_of_philippine_forests.html), snippet).

### Quantifying and showing uncertainty

With independent errors, σ_GI = √(Σ wᵢ²σᵢ²). Illustrative σ values of 8, 5 and 12 points give about ±10 points at 95%. Canopy and vegetation errors are positively correlated, though, so the Monte Carlo in step 7 is the method of record. It should draw each fraction from Beta(p, n) using the validation n where one exists, and use a wide prior otherwise. This follows the OECD/JRC practice of testing robustness to weighting and normalisation choices ([Tarantola et al.](https://typeset.io/pdf/uncertainty-and-sensitivity-analysis-techniques-as-tools-for-58wdclclq0.pdf); [OECD 2024](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/government-at-a-glance-latin-america-and-the-caribbean-2024_4abdba16-en/full-report/component-76)).

Before local validation exists, default confidence chips should be: canopy from CHMv2 **Moderate→Low** (the ±2.8 m MAE of v1 sits near the height threshold, and there is no Asian validation; [Tolan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07213v3)); WorldCover fractions **Moderate**; NDVI **High as a measurement, Moderate as a proxy**; sealed share **Low**, the weakest component, because no product maps in-park paving. Places whose ranges overlap substantially are shown as ties, not ranks.

### Labelling under the glass-box rules

| Label | When it applies | What the UI shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Estimate** | Default for every modelled value: T, G, W, S, NDVI and the composite GI | Method version, source and dataset version, imagery date, ± range, confidence chip |
| **Estimate · checked** | Product value photo-interpreted with n ≥ 100 and agreeing within the CI. Badge expires after 24 months or when visible change appears | "Checked by [person] on [date] against [imagery, date], n = X, ±Y pp" |
| **Verified** | Only a fact copied from an authoritative document and confirmed by a person, e.g. an official park area in ha or an LGU tree-inventory count. **Never the composite.** | Citation, document date, "last checked" date |
| **Not available** | Missing or ambiguous polygon; `n_clear` below threshold after fallback; unit under 0.25 ha for 10 m products; any component that cannot be computed. Never impute from neighbours. | Reason code |

Re-validation runs every year on a fresh random 10–20% of places, plus any place a user flags or whose value moves by more than 10 pp. A change counts as material when |Δ| > max(0.05 NDVI, 2·√(u₁² + u₂²)), or more than 5 pp for canopy. These thresholds are heuristics, not literature values.

### Main risks

CHMv2 is untested in tropical cities. v1 underestimated urban trees in London ([VoxCity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13934)) and can confuse roofs with canopy, which is why footprints are masked. Canopy data may be several years stale. WorldCover is frozen at 2021. The sealed-surface term depends on OSM paving completeness, which is unknown for NCR. Euclidean buffers overstate access. Weight choices are conventions that only sensitivity analysis can defend. Finally, publishing any number before step 9 would repeat the current seed's error of a confident label on unvalidated data.

## Conclusion

The largest gain in defensibility comes from changing what the score *is*, more than from better satellites. EQ-001 averaged three overlapping proxies under a borrowed citation. EQ-002 is a target-referenced statement about mutually exclusive ground cover, so each component reads as a plain fact about a place ("12% tree canopy, target 30%") that a resident or reviewer can check point-by-point against imagery. That turns validation from an academic exercise into the publication gate. It also means the most valuable near-term work is a weekend of labelling about 2,600 random points, not more processing.

A second implication is that Lunhaw's "Verified" label will be rare for green metrics, and that is a strength. Metro Manila's own benchmarks disagree by a factor of five or more depending on definition, from roughly 3% tree area to 21% green space. A product that reports Estimates with ranges, dates and definitions will be more trustworthy than the official-sounding figures around it. Immediate priority: purge the simulated seed that currently claims `VERIFIED` Copernicus provenance, because no methodology can be defended while that row exists.
